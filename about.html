Our Mission

Psychologists for Perinatal Mental Health and Reproductive Justice (PMHRJ) is a network of psychologists, trainees, and allied professionals working to make perinatal mental health a visible, resourced focus within psychology, grounded in a reproductive justice framework.

Mental health conditions are a leading cause of maternal death in the United States, and the burden falls hardest on Black, Indigenous, Latine, immigrant, and low-income birthing people. Yet psychology has no dedicated professional home for this work. Perinatal training is something psychologists largely piece together on their own, the research communities live mostly in psychiatry, and reproductive justice is rarely centered in either. PMHRJ exists to change that.

We believe mental health care during pregnancy, birth, postpartum, and across the reproductive lifespan is inseparable from bodily autonomy, equitable access to care, and the dismantling of the systemic barriers that harm marginalized communities.

Our Framework

We root our work in reproductive justice, a framework created by Black feminist activists in 1994: the right to have children, the right to not have children, and the right to parent in safe and healthy communities. For PMHRJ, that means our scope spans the full intersection of perinatal mental health and reproductive justice, including mood and anxiety disorders, PTSD and trauma, substance use, loss and infertility, and matrescence alongside birth equity, abortion access, the criminalization of pregnancy, immigration and care access, and the policies shaping who gets to parent with dignity.

What We Do

Connection

Our member directory and listserv help psychologists and trainees find each other for collaboration on grants, manuscripts, symposia, mentorship, and referrals, by career stage, institution, and area of focus.

Resource Sharing

We surface training opportunities, research, and practical information our members need, including who is taking students, where training gaps exist, and who is doing this work well.

Advocacy Through Training

We work to build perinatal mental health and reproductive justice competency into psychology training, from graduate coursework to internship to specialty practice, so this work becomes a visible, resourced part of the field rather than something trainees have to seek out on their own.

Our Values

Reproductive Justice

Not adjacent to our work, but the ground it stands on.

Equity

We center Black, Indigenous, Latine, immigrant, LGBTQ+, and other communities historically underserved by perinatal mental health systems and excluded from psychology's own ranks.

Evidence and Lived Experience

We promote clinical practice, research, and policy grounded in science, lived experience, and community accountability.

Collective Action

Lasting change happens through collaboration across career stages, disciplines, and the communities we serve. Trainees and students are full members here, not spectators.

Who We Are

Launched in 2026, PMHRJ has grown to a network of 165+ psychologists, trainees, and allied professionals across clinical, counseling, developmental, and health psychology, alongside members in nursing, medicine, birth work, and related fields. Our members work in academic medical centers, universities, hospitals, community organizations, and private practices across the United States and internationally.

The network is guided by a founding steering committee and organized around working groups focused on connection, resource sharing, and training-focused advocacy.

PMHRJ was founded by Dr. Christin A. Mujica, a bilingual Venezuelan American clinical psychologist and researcher whose work focuses on oppression-based trauma, perinatal mental health, substance use, and health equity with BIPOC and Latina communities.
